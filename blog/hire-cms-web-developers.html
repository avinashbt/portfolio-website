1. What a CMS Web Developer Delivers

When you hire CMS web developers, you are hiring for the full website — not just a template. They build the theme, page templates, navigation, forms, and content structure so editors can update pages without a developer. They also handle the parts that decide whether the site succeeds: page speed, mobile layout, SEO markup and accessibility.

The difference between a $500 template install and a professional CMS build shows up in load time, Core Web Vitals, and how quickly your marketing team ships a new landing page.

2. Skills Checklist

  • CMS platforms: WordPress, Webflow, Drupal, Shopify or headless CMS
  • Frontend: HTML5, CSS3, responsive layout, JavaScript, Bootstrap or Tailwind
  • SEO on-page: semantic markup, schema, sitemaps, redirects, clean URLs
  • Performance: image optimization, caching, CDN, mobile speed
  • Integrations: forms, CRM, email, analytics, payment gateways
  • Handover: documentation, training and backups your team can rely on

5. How to Vet Before You Hire

  • Open three of their live sites — test on mobile, not desktop screenshots
  • Run each through PageSpeed Insights — under 80 on mobile is a warning sign
  • Ask who owns the domain, hosting and CMS accounts after handover
  • Request a short paid discovery before the full contract

Red flags: no live links, all projects look identical, no mention of backups, or a quote without a scope document. These almost always lead to scope creep.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

What does a CMS web developer do?

They build the site on a content management system — templates, responsive layouts, forms, SEO structure and performance — so your team can edit pages visually.

How much does a CMS website cost?

$2,000–$8,000 for a small business site, $8,000–$20,000 for a 10–30 page marketing site, and $20,000+ for e-commerce or custom apps.

Build or retainer?

Hire project-based for the build, then keep a retainer for updates, backups and security — it costs less than ad-hoc fixes later.

How long does it take?

2–3 weeks for a small site, 4–8 weeks for a medium site, 8–16 weeks for complex e-commerce.
